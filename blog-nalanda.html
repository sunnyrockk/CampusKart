What Nalanda can still teach us about learning

A centuries-old centre of scholarship offers a surprisingly modern model for curious students.

For centuries, Nalanda was one of the best-known learning centres in the world. Scholars travelled there to study subjects ranging from logic and language to medicine and philosophy. Its story reminds us that meaningful education has never been only about memorising answers.

Learning was a shared activity

Ideas grew through conversation. Students challenged one another, questioned teachers and compared different traditions of thought. That same habit can improve a modern study session: explain an idea to a friend, ask where the evidence comes from, and welcome a question that makes you reconsider.

Curiosity needs a home

The famous library represents more than a collection of books. It represents access: a place where people could encounter ideas beyond their immediate experience. Today, libraries, study groups, open courses and thoughtful online communities can play that role.

Try this today

Pick one topic from class and write down three questions you genuinely want to answer. Begin your next study session with the questions, not the chapter heading.
